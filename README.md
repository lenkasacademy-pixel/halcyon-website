# Halcyon Pain Management Centre — website

Static site for Halcyon Pain Management Centre, Kukatpally, Hyderabad. It is built to
replace the WordPress site at **https://halcyonpainfree.com/**, and every page keeps the
address the old site ranked with, so existing Google rankings carry over.

Preview (until the domain moves): https://lenkasacademy-pixel.github.io/halcyon-website/

Old addresses redirect to their new equivalent: the flat `Halcyon-*.html` files this
prototype used to publish, `shoulder-pain.html`, and the old WordPress pages that have no
page of the same name (`/book-an-appointment/`, `/our-practice/`, the four blog posts, the
ad landing pages …). Each is a tiny page with an instant redirect, `noindex` and a
canonical pointing at the new address. `Halcyon-YourVisit-demo.html` is an old demo, kept

Images and fonts live in `assets/` (images are WebP with descriptive file names). Pages
link with relative paths, so the site works both at the domain root and on the preview
URL. To look at it locally, serve the folder rather than opening files directly:
`python3 -m http.server` then http://localhost:8000/.

## The search layer — `tools/build.py`

Run `python3 tools/build.py` after changing anything in `tools/content/` or the facts at
the top of `tools/build.py`; then `python3 tools/check.py`, which must report 0 errors
before publishing. The build is safe to re-run. It:

- renders the 19 generated pages in the site's own design (header, footer, hero, CTA and
  scripts are lifted from `services/index.html`, so they never drift from it);
- rewrites the block between `<!-- seo:start -->` and `<!-- seo:end -->` in every page's
  `<head>`: title, description, canonical, robots, hreflang, Open Graph and Twitter cards,
  geo tags, icons, font preloads, and a JSON-LD graph (MedicalClinic, WebSite, page type,
  BreadcrumbList, MedicalCondition / TherapeuticProcedure / Person, FAQPage). **Edit the
  build, not the block** — hand edits inside it are overwritten;
- keeps the hand-made pages' extras in place: the hub cards' links to topic pages, the
  home page FAQ (between `<!-- faq:start -->` / `<!-- faq:end -->`), the first patient
  story rendered into the HTML, image dimensions and alt text;
- writes `sitemap.xml` (with images), `robots.txt` (every search engine and AI crawler
  allowed, by name), `llms.txt` and `llms-full.txt` (the site in plain text for AI
  assistants), `site.webmanifest` and `404.html`;
- generates the 1200×630 share images in `assets/og/` when they are missing (delete one
  to regenerate it). This step needs `pip install -r tools/requirements.txt`.

The clinic's name, address, phone and hours are defined once, in `SITE` at the top of
`tools/build.py`. The phone number must stay identical to the Google Business Profile
(+91 77880 91092).

## The assistant and lead capture

Every page has the assistant (the "Ask about a condition…" bar). It is static — no AI
service behind it — and lives in three files:

| File | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| `assets/js/assistant.js` | The engine and the chat panel. Matching: synonyms (Telugu/Hindi words typed in English, misspellings) → spell-correction → key phrases + BM25 over example questions → answer, answer-with-alternatives, or "did you mean". Red-flag symptoms always win. |
| `tools/content/assistant.json` | The hand-written knowledge base: ~200 entries (logistics, procedures, safety, doctors, every condition and sub-condition, honest "we don't treat that" answers, emergency red flags, small talk). **Edit this to change what it says.** |
| `tools/content/assistant.te-1.json`, `.te-2.json` | The Telugu layer for those entries: ~1,400 Telugu/Hindi phrasings and one Telugu summary line (`te`) per entry. |
| `tools/content/assistant.te-words.json` | ~990 Telugu/Hindi words mapped to English, ~490 more English misspellings, and the assistant's own lines in Telugu (`ui`). |
| `assets/js/assistant-kb.js` | Generated by `tools/build.py` from the file above **plus every FAQ on the 18 topic pages**, so the assistant and the pages never disagree. Don't edit it. |

After changing any of those, run `python3 tools/build.py` and then
`node tools/test_assistant.js` — **529 real-world questions** (English, Telugu and Hindi in
English letters, Telugu script, typos, emergencies, and ordinary worries that must *not* be
treated as emergencies) that must land on the right answer. The Telugu cases live in the
`tests` array of each `assistant.te-*.json`. Add a line whenever you fix a wrong answer.

**Telugu and Hindi.** Most patients type Telugu in English letters, and nobody spells it
the same way twice. The engine handles this in three steps: Telugu and Devanagari script
are transliterated to roman (మోకాలి నొప్పి → "mokaali noppi"); a phonetic fold strips the
differences that carry no meaning — doubled letters, long vowels, aspirated consonants,
the trailing vowel — so mokali / mokaali / mokallu / mokkali collapse to one key; and a
word list (`indic` in the `assistant.te-*.json` files) maps Telugu and Hindi words to the
English the entries use. Every question is scored both as typed and folded, and the better
score wins. When someone writes in Telugu, each answer carries a one-line Telugu summary
(`te` on the entry) above the English, and the assistant's own questions show their Telugu
line from `ui`. Those Telugu lines are written by people, not machine-translated at
runtime — **the clinic should read them once before launch** (`tools/content/assistant.te-*.json`).

**The conversation.** It asks where it hurts (skipped on a condition page, which already
answers that), for how long, which scans, then a name, a 10-digit Indian mobile and a
good time to call. Questions are answered at any point and the thread picks up again;
"call me back", "book an appointment" or simply typing a number jumps straight to the
contact details; "I just have a question" skips the intake. The patient sees a summary
card and must press **Send to the team** — that press is the consent, and the card says
what it is for. The conversation survives moving between pages in the same tab.

**Where leads go — one-time setup (about 5 minutes, clinic's Google account):**

1. Create a Google Sheet, then Extensions → Apps Script; paste `tools/lead-collector.gs`.
2. Optionally set `NOTIFY_EMAIL` at the top (default: the Sheet owner's Gmail).
3. Run the `setup` function once and approve the permissions.
4. Deploy → New deployment → Web app · Execute as **Me** · Access **Anyone** → copy the URL.
5. Paste it into `SITE['lead_endpoint']` in `tools/build.py`, run `python3 tools/build.py`,
   commit and push.

Until step 5 the assistant still collects the details but hands them to WhatsApp instead
of sending them. Each lead becomes a row with a **Status** dropdown for the front desk
(New → Called → Booked → Visited …) and an email alert. A failed send is kept in the
visitor's browser and retried.

**Which ad brings leads.** The assistant records how each visitor arrived — `utm_source`,
`utm_medium`, `utm_campaign`, `utm_content`, `utm_term`, and Google (`gclid`) / Meta
(`fbclid`) click ids — from the first page they land on, for 90 days, and sends it with
the lead. The Sheet's **By source** tab counts leads per source and campaign, bookings per
campaign, leads per week and what people ask about. For this to work, tag every ad's
final URL, e.g.
`https://halcyonpainfree.com/knee-pain-treatment/?utm_source=google&utm_medium=cpc&utm_campaign=knee-hyd-oct&utm_content=ad1`
(Google Ads auto-tagging adds `gclid` by itself; in Meta Ads put the same parameters in
"URL parameters").

**Privacy.** Section 3 of the privacy policy now describes exactly what the assistant
sends and stores; the retention period is a placeholder for the clinic to decide.

## Deploying

The site is live at **https://halcyonpainfree.com/**, hosted on **Hostinger**. It is
plain files — no PHP, no database — so it runs on ordinary web hosting.

**Every push to `main` publishes it.** The `Publish the site` workflow rebuilds,
diffs the result against what is committed, runs `check.py` over 50 pages and the
529 assistant tests, and only then uploads over FTPS. If any check fails, nothing
is published.

To see what a deploy would do without touching the server: Actions → **Publish the
site** → Run workflow → tick **dry run**. It prints the file counts and, crucially,
how much it would delete. Do this after changing any FTP setting.

### Settings

| where | name | value |
|---|---|---|
| Secrets | `FTP_SERVER` | the **FTP IP** from hPanel → Files → FTP Accounts |
| Secrets | `FTP_USERNAME` | e.g. `u0000000.deploy` |
| Secrets | `FTP_PASSWORD` | its password |
| Variables | `FTP_DIR` | `./` |
| Variables | `DEPLOY` | `true` |

`FTP_SERVER` must be the **IP**, not `ftp.halcyonpainfree.com` — that hostname has no
DNS record and never resolves, which is what broke the first two deploy attempts, on
both hosts. `FTP_DIR` is `./` because the FTP account is rooted at the site's own
`public_html`.

### The carried pages

`7788.html`, `8585.html` and `7272.html` are the landing pages the Meta ads point at,
plus `home.html`, `layout-styles.css` and `404.shtml` alongside them. They came from
the old WordPress site and are **not part of this site** — they are standalone pages
that post to their own Apps Script endpoint.

They live in this repo so they deploy wherever the site deploys. When the nameservers
moved to Hostinger they were briefly off the air, because they existed only on the old
server; carrying them here means that cannot happen again. `check.py` lists them under
`CARRIED` and skips them: they have no canonical, no schema and no Open Graph, and
they are not supposed to.

### If you ever move hosts again

1. Point the new host at the domain and get a certificate.
2. Change `FTP_SERVER` / `FTP_USERNAME` / `FTP_PASSWORD`, and `FTP_DIR` if the account
   is not rooted at the document root.
3. Dry-run. Check `Deleting:` reads `0 B`.
4. Run it for real, then verify: the homepage, a deep page, all three landing pages,
   `http →  https`, `www → non-www`, and that no `X-Robots-Tag: noindex` leaked onto
   the live host.

The old WordPress remains untouched on the previous host, so reverting nameservers
brings it back.

## Launch checklist

1. **Point the domain here.** Add a `CNAME` file containing `halcyonpainfree.com`, set the
   custom domain in the repo's GitHub Pages settings, and change the DNS records at the
   registrar to GitHub Pages. Do this only when ready to switch — the moment `CNAME`
   exists, the preview URL starts redirecting to the domain. `robots.txt` and
   `sitemap.xml` only take effect once the site is at the domain root.
2. **Google Search Console — verify by DNS *before* the switch.** The old WordPress site
   carries no verification tag and the domain has no `google-site-verification` TXT record,
   so today's verification almost certainly rides on Google Site Kit or the Analytics tag —
   both of which disappear with the old site. In Search Console add the **Domain** property
   `halcyonpainfree.com`, copy the TXT record it gives you into the registrar's DNS, and
   verify. That method survives any change of site or host. (If you would rather use the
   HTML tag method, paste its content value into `SITE['google_site_verification']` in
   `tools/build.py` and rebuild.) Then submit `https://halcyonpainfree.com/sitemap.xml`, and
   use URL Inspection → Request indexing on the home page and the 12 condition pages. Watch
   Pages → "Not found (404)" for a few weeks for any old URL that still needs a redirect.
3. **Bing Webmaster Tools:** import from Search Console and submit the sitemap. Bing's
   index also feeds ChatGPT search and Copilot.
4. **Google Business Profile:** set the website to `https://halcyonpainfree.com/` and the
   appointment link to `https://halcyonpainfree.com/contact/`; keep name, address, phone
   and hours identical to the site.
5. **Test:** paste a few URLs into https://search.google.com/test/rich-results and
   https://validator.schema.org/.
6. **Ads:** the old `/pain-relief-ad1/` and `/pain-relief-ad2/` landing pages now redirect
   to the home page. Point live ad campaigns at a condition page instead.

## Measurement and cookies

Google Tag Manager (`GTM-PBQT2N67`) loads on **every page view** —
`assets/js/analytics.js`, container id in `SITE['gtm_id']`. There is no cookie banner.

It was built the other way first, gated behind an Accept button, and that was changed
deliberately: only the visitors who pressed Accept were being counted, which made the
numbers worse than useless. The one exception kept is a browser already asking not to be
tracked — **Do Not Track or Global Privacy Control, and the tag does not load at all**.

Analytics lives *inside* Tag Manager: add the GA4 tag (`G-TV4ZWHCQWL`) to the container
once, and anything else (Google Ads conversions, Meta pixel) the same way, with no code
change here. **List whatever you switch on in the "Cookies and measurement" part of the
privacy policy**, which carries a placeholder for it.

That last point is not bookkeeping. The policy describes what the code does, in writing,
to patients. If `analytics.js` changes, that section changes in the same commit — a
privacy policy that does not match the code is worse than none.

India's DPDP Act is the law that applies here, and this is a clinic. Whether notice-only
measurement is the right posture is a question for whoever advises the practice, not a
question this file can settle.

## Before this goes live

1. **Medical copy needs the doctors' review.** The 18 topic pages were written from the
   clinic's own copy, but they add standard explanations and a few specific statements
   that should be confirmed:
   - knee radiofrequency is described as following a diagnostic block of the genicular nerves;
   - prolotherapy as "most commonly a concentrated dextrose solution";
   - hydrodissection fluid as "usually saline, sometimes with local anaesthetic or dextrose";
     nerve blocks as "usually a local anaesthetic and sometimes a steroid";
   - trigeminal neuralgia RF as "image-guided" and repeatable, with numbness as a possible effect;
   - hip is linked only to regenerative medicine; shoulder and sciatica/slipped disc to the
     nerve-blocks page (suprascapular block, epidural / transforaminal injection);
   - the doctors' profiles describe their approach from the site's own copy — no years of
     experience, past hospitals or colleges are stated.
   Once reviewed, a visible "Medically reviewed by …" line and `reviewedBy` /
   `lastReviewed` in the schema are worth adding — they are strong trust signals for
   health pages. They are deliberately absent until a review has happened.
2. **The privacy policy is a working draft.** It carries a visible "not yet reviewed"
   notice and highlighted placeholders for decisions only the clinic can make. It is
   written against India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 but is not legal
   advice and needs a lawyer's review.
3. **Social profiles.** `sameAs` in the schema lists the Facebook, Instagram, LinkedIn,
   YouTube and X accounts linked from the old site. The old site linked two accounts on
   some networks; keep only the ones the clinic still runs. The footer's social icons
   still point nowhere.
